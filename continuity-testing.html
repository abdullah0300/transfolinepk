  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

What is Electrical Continuity Testing?

Continuity testing is the precision measurement of low electrical resistance across conducting paths, protective bonding conductors, and metallic joints. By injecting a high DC test current (10A to 100A) using the 4-wire Kelvin bridge method, our engineers detect micro-resistance anomalies that cause catastrophic thermal runaway under full operating load.

01

Busbar Splice Micro-Ohm Verification

Measures resistance across overlapping copper/aluminum busbar joints. Guarantees that joint resistance is equal to or lower than the adjacent solid copper conductor to prevent contact burning.

02

Protective Bonding Continuity

Verifies low-resistance bonds (≤ 0.1Ω) between equipment metallic enclosures, cable trays, structural steel, and the main earthing terminal to ensure instantaneous breaker tripping during ground faults.

Continuity & Micro-Ohm Testing Applications

CONT.01 Switchgear Busbar Joint Resistance (Ductor Test) Four-wire Kelvin measurement across all main and riser busbar bolted joints in HT and LT distribution switchboards. Busbar Joints

04   CONTINUITY FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions.

Expert guidance on low resistance micro-ohm testing.

DEFECTS What happens if a busbar joint shows high micro-ohm resistance?

High resistance indicates surface oxidation, inadequate bolt torque, or poor contact surface mating. We unbolt the joint, wire-brush contact surfaces, apply conductive anti-oxidation grease, and re-torque with calibrated torque wrenches before re-testing.

INSTRUMENTS Which micro-ohmmeters does TransfoLine use?

We utilize calibrated Megger DLRO10HD and Fluke 1587 high-precision low-resistance micro-ohmmeters capable of measuring down to 0.1 micro-ohms (μΩ) with four-wire Kelvin test leads.
